    Check yourself

    You hold 1 GC contract and gold drops $12 against you. What's your loss?

    GC pays $100 for every $1 move, so $12 × $100 = $1,200. The same move on one MGC is $120. That's why MGC is the sizing tool for prop accounts.

    RULE

    Know how many dollars of gold movement your daily limit can absorb before you place a trade. If it's less than a normal hour's range, you're too big.

    WHY

    A prop account usually dies on the daily loss limit, not on a wrong idea. Gold can move $10–20 in minutes around news. If your limit only survives a $10 move, normal noise can end your day before your idea has a chance to work.

    Step 2 of 12 · Fundamentals & macro

    Gold is priced against what it costs to hold it

    Gold pays no interest. When safe assets like Treasuries pay a high real return, holding gold is expensive. When that return falls, or fear rises, gold gets bid.

    Check yourself

    Real yields jump 25bp and the dollar rallies, but gold still closes higher. What's the best read?

    When gold rises against its two main headwinds, someone big is buying anyway (often central banks or haven flows). Professionals treat that as a sign of strength, not an error.

    RULE

    Every Sunday, write one sentence: "This month gold is mainly trading ____." Trade in line with that driver.

    WHY

    Gold's drivers take turns being in charge. If the market is trading central-bank buying and you're watching real yields, your signals will point the wrong way. Naming the driver tells you which chart to trust this month.

    Step 3 of 12 · Intermarket analysis

    Ask other markets whether they agree

    A gold move backed by the dollar, yields, silver and miners is high quality. A move that other markets don't confirm is suspect. And a move against its usual headwinds is the loudest signal of all.

    Check yourself

    Gold breaks to a new weekly high, but silver and gold miners (GDX) are both red on the day. What do you do?

    Silver and miners usually join a real gold breakout, and miners often lead at turning points. When they refuse, the breakout has a higher failure rate. You don't have to short it, just lower your trust.

    RULE

    Watch divergences more than correlations. Gold up against a strong dollar = strength. Gold flat on a weak-dollar day = weakness.

    WHY

    When gold rises against something that normally pushes it down, a buyer is strong enough to absorb that pressure. That's information you can't see on gold's chart alone, and it often shows up before the price move does.

    Step 4 of 12 · Volume, open interest & positioning

    Is real money behind the move?

    Price tells you what happened. Volume tells you how hard people pushed. Open interest tells you whether new money came in or old money left. Together they separate trends you can ride from moves that fade.

    Learn

    Two numbers that aren't the same

    Volume

    How many contracts changed hands today. It measures activity and urgency. It resets every session.

    Open interest (OI)

    How many contracts are still open at the end of the day. It rises when new positions open and falls when positions close. It carries over from day to day.

    Learn

    Volume profile: where the market agreed on price

    A volume profile turns volume sideways: how many contracts traded at each price. It shows where the market did business (fair value) and where it rushed through (unfair prices).

    • POC (point of control): the price with the most volume. It pulls price back like a magnet.
    • Value area (VAH to VAL): where 70% of volume traded. Its edges often act as support and resistance.
    • HVN / LVN: high-volume nodes slow price down. Low-volume nodes are "air pockets" that price crosses fast.
    • VWAP: the average price paid today, weighted by volume. Longs are winning above it, shorts below it.

    Practical notes
    • CME posts daily volume and OI after the session, and final OI the next business day. Use OI for your daily and weekly bias, not for minute-by-minute entries.
    • During roll weeks, volume and OI move from the old contract to the new one. Read the total across months, or only the most active contract.
    • GC/MGC futures volume is real exchange volume. CFD and spot-forex "tick volume" isn't, so don't mix them.
    • A huge volume spike after a long trend, with a long wick, often marks exhaustion (a climax), not a new trend.

    Check yourself

    Gold rallies $45 on heavy volume, but open interest falls by 9,000 contracts. What's most likely happening?

    Price up + OI down = positions closing on the buy side, which means shorts are covering. Heavy volume makes it sharp, but no new money arrived. Take profits faster and wait for OI to rise before trusting it as a trend.

    Price pushes above yesterday's value-area high, then two 30-minute bars close back inside value. What's the most likely next move?

    The market tested higher prices and rejected them (a failed breakout). Rejected auctions often travel back through value toward the POC. That gives you a trade idea with a clear stop above the high.

    RULE

    Trust trends where price and open interest rise together on above-average volume. Fade or avoid moves driven only by short covering or liquidation.

    WHY

    Rising open interest means new money has committed to the move and has a reason to defend it. Falling open interest means the move is just people closing old positions, and it runs out of fuel once they've finished.

    Step 5 of 12 · News events

    Trade the surprise, not the headline

    Markets price in the consensus forecast ahead of time. Gold reacts to the gap between actual and expected, and to what that gap does to rate expectations.

    Watch these during a release

    Check yourself

    Core CPI prints 0.4% vs. 0.3% expected. Gold spikes up $15 in the first minute, then the 2-yr yield and the dollar climb steadily for 20 minutes. What's the professional read?

    Hot inflation means fewer cuts, which pushes yields and the dollar up and weighs on gold. The yield and dollar move that holds is your confirmation. The first-minute spike was stop orders getting hit.

    RULE

    No trades in the first 2 minutes. Let the 2-yr yield and the dollar tell you which way the surprise really cut.

    WHY

    In the first seconds after a release, orders disappear and computer programs trade the headline number, so price whips both ways and stops fill at bad prices. The direction that survives shows what big money decided after reading the details.

    Step 6 of 12 · Historical events

    History doesn't repeat, but the regimes do

    Each era of gold was ruled by one or two drivers. When today starts to look like one of them, you already know how gold behaved last time.

    Learn

    Gold since 1971: tap an era

    Log scale. Dots are approximate spot-price milestones, and the line only connects them. It isn't a full price history.

    Try it · Match the analog

    Stocks crash 10% in a week, margin calls spread, and gold falls 5% alongside stocks. Which era does this echo?

    In a liquidity panic, gold gets sold to raise cash first. In both 2008 and 2020 it then recovered to new highs once central banks flooded the system. Wait for the selling to exhaust before you buy.

    The Fed signals it will slow its support, real yields jump fast from negative toward positive, and gold's rally stalls. Which era?

    In 2013 rising real-yield expectations ended gold's decade-long bull, with a crash in April. A fast real-yield rise is the classic trend killer.

    Inflation stays high, the Fed hikes hard, yet gold makes new highs as central banks buy record amounts. Which era?

    From 2022 the usual "high real yields = weak gold" link loosened because official-sector buying became the main driver. That's why the weekly question "which driver is in charge?" matters.

    RULE

    Keep an "analog file". When today's setup resembles a past era, reread how gold behaved and what ended that move.

    WHY

    Markets are people reacting to fear, forced selling and central-bank policy, and people react to similar situations in similar ways. Past eras show how far moves can go and, just as useful, what usually ended them.

    Step 7 of 12 · Seasonal cycles

    Seasonality is a tailwind, never the engine

    Some months have historically been firmer for gold, often because physical buying follows the calendar. Use it to add conviction when macro already agrees.

    Check yourself

    It's January, historically a firm month. But the Fed just turned hawkish, real yields are surging and the dollar is breaking out. How much weight does seasonality get?

    Seasonality only adds weight when the bigger drivers are neutral or agree. Against a clear macro headwind it's noise. In the scorecard (step 8) it carries the smallest weight.

    RULE

    Use seasonality only to break a tie or to add size when macro, intermarket and volume already agree.

    WHY

    Seasonal patterns come from physical buying, like jewellery and festivals, which is small next to the money moved by interest rates and central banks. They're also averages over many years, so any single year can do the opposite.

    Check yourself

    Your scorecard reads +12, and the drivers are split: yields bullish, dollar bearish, OI falling. What's the plan?

    A small score with conflicting drivers means the market has no clear direction. Sitting out protects your drawdown for the weeks when the score is strong.

    RULE

    Only trade against your weekly bias if price breaks a major weekly level and volume and OI confirm the break. Otherwise, standing aside is a position.

    WHY

    Most counter-trend trades fight the big money that's driving the trend. A weekly level breaking while open interest rises is evidence that big money has actually changed sides, and that's the only time it's worth fighting your bias.

    Step 9 of 12 · Execution for a prop account

    Size so you survive long enough to get paid

    Payouts come from staying inside the drawdown while your edge plays out. Size every trade from your stop distance, never from how confident you feel.

    Your routine checklist

    Check yourself

    Your buffer is $2,000 and you risk 10% ($200). The 15-minute ATR is $8 and you use a 1.5 × ATR stop on MGC. How many contracts?

    Stop = 1.5 × $8 = $12. On MGC that's $120 per contract. Two contracts would risk $240, which is over $200, so the answer is 1. Round down, never up.

    RULE

    Stop trading for the day after 2 full losses or 3× your per-trade risk. The next session is where the payout comes from.

    WHY

    Position size, not being right, decides whether you survive a losing streak. After two losses people tend to trade bigger and faster to win it back (revenge trading). That's how daily limits get hit.

    Step 10 of 12 · The feedback loop

    Your journal turns trades into an edge

    Record the macro context with every trade. After 50–100 trades you'll see which regimes, setups and times of day actually pay you, and you can cut everything else.

    Try it

    Journal entry

    Learn

    What to look for after 50+ trades

    By regime

    Do you make money when the bias score is above +40 and lose when it's near zero? Then stop trading near zero.

    By session

    London open, 8:30 data, or the afternoon: which session pays you? Trade that one.

    By volume/OI read

    Compare trades with rising OI to trades in short-covering moves. That comparison tells you how much weight to put on step 4.

    By plan-following

    Compare "Yes" trades with "No" trades. The gap is the money your discipline makes.

    Check yourself

    After 60 journaled trades, what's the most valuable thing to do with the data?

    Your edge is usually a small subset of what you trade. Cutting the losing contexts raises your win rate and smooths your equity curve, which is exactly what prop consistency rules reward.

    Your one-page playbook
    1. Size in MGC. Know how many dollars of movement your daily limit absorbs.
    2. Name the driver in charge every Sunday.
    3. Demand confirmation from the dollar, yields, silver and miners. Treat divergences as signals.
    4. Ride moves where price and OI rise together on above-average volume. Distrust short covering.
    5. Respect the calendar. No trades in the first 2 minutes of a Tier 1 release.
    6. Match today to a historical regime and remember how that one ended.
    7. Use seasonality as a tie-breaker only.
    8. Score the drivers. A weak score means smaller size or no trade.
    9. Size from an ATR-based stop. Stop for the day after 2 losses.
    10. Journal everything, review every 50 trades, and cut what doesn't pay.
    RULE

    Journal every trade. Every 50 trades, keep the setups that pay and drop the ones that don't.

    WHY

    Memory lies: you remember big wins and forget the small losses you keep repeating. A written record shows which situations actually pay you, so you can do more of those and cut the rest.

    Step 11 of 12 · How to keep up without drowning

    Your trading week, one picture at a time

    New to trading? This lesson answers two questions: how often you check the macro drivers, and how you decide without reading everything. Go through the 10 slides in order. Each one tells you what to do and why.

    Slide 1 of 10

    First, why gold moves at all

    Every rule in this course comes from one chain reaction. Once you understand it, you'll know why you check the dollar and the 2-yr yield and ignore most headlines. Pick what the market starts to expect, then step through each link.

    Try it: what does holding gold cost you?
    When the chain breaks

    Some buyers don't care about bond yields. Central banks buy gold for safety, for example to hold reserves that can't be frozen by sanctions. From 2022, their buying was big enough that gold rose even while real yields were high.

    That's why you check every week which driver is in charge. The chain still pulls on gold, but sometimes a bigger buyer pulls harder.

    Think of it like a road trip

    You don't re-plan a road trip every minute. You choose the route once, check traffic before you leave, then keep your eyes on the road. Trading gold works the same way.

    Do
    • Plan your direction once a week
    • Do a quick check each morning
    • Watch only the chart while trading
    Skip
    • Reading every headline
    • Checking the dollar every 5 minutes
    • Changing your mind because of one tweet
    WHY
    • Macro tells you the direction big money is likely pushing gold. The chart tells you the timing: where to get in and out.
    • Macro drivers change slowly. The Fed meets 8 times a year and most economic data comes out once a month. Price changes every second.
    • If you check slow things every few minutes, normal wiggles look like news. You start changing your mind and over-trading, and that's how prop accounts get blown. So check each thing at the speed it actually changes.

    Rest

    WHY
    • Sunday: the market is closed, so price can't scare or excite you while you decide. Calm decisions are better decisions.
    • Every morning: Asia and London trade while you sleep. The dollar or yields may already have moved, and you want to know before you risk money.
    • News times: these are the only scheduled moments when new information arrives, so they're the only times macro can change fast.
    • Only 1½ hours: there just isn't more new information than that in a normal week.

    Sunday: pick your direction

    Once a week, with the market closed, you decide which way you'll trade. Four small steps:

    1Look at 3 charts Real yield, dollar, 2-yr yield. Going down is good for gold. Going up is bad.
    2Fill in the scorecard
    BearishNeutralBullish
    Rate each of the 10 drivers in step 8. About 20 minutes.
    3Read the meter
    It says Long, Short, or No edge. No edge means trade small or not at all.
    4Write one sentence
    "Gold is trading falling real yields. Bias: long. I change my mind if the 2-yr closes above last week's high."
    Keep it next to your screen. This is what you check during the week.
    WHY
    • Why these 3 charts: they measure what it costs to hold gold (see slide 1). When they fall, holding gold gets cheaper and big money tends to buy.
    • Why a written sentence: you decide while calm, so you don't decide while scared in the middle of a trade.
    • Why "I change my mind if…": it stops two mistakes. You won't cling to a dead idea, and you won't flip on every wiggle. You only change when your own condition is hit.

    Every morning: three numbers, three lights

    Before you trade, look at how far these three moved since yesterday's close. You only worry if they moved against your bias. Try it: type in this morning's moves.

    1 bp (basis point) = 0.01%. If the 2-yr yield goes from 3.50% to 3.58%, that's +8 bp.

    WHY
    • Why only moves against you: a move in your favour doesn't threaten your plan. Only a move against it could mean your reason for the trade is gone.
    • Why these limits: on a normal day the dollar moves a few tenths of a percent and the 2-yr yield a few basis points. A move far bigger than normal usually means real new information, not noise.
    • Why half size on amber: when you're less sure, you risk less. It's the same logic as driving slower in fog.

    While trading: only the chart

    Once the session starts, the macro work is done. Your screen should show gold, your levels, VWAP and volume. Hide or ignore the macro charts until a news release or an alert.

    Watch
    • Price at your marked levels
    • Above or below VWAP
    • Is volume bigger than usual?
    • Your stop and your daily loss limit
    Ignore
    • News feeds and social media
    • Other people's opinions
    • The macro charts, unless an alert fires
    WHY
    • Macro barely changes during a normal day, but price does. Watching both splits your attention.
    • By the time a headline is written, big traders have usually acted already, and your chart shows their buying or selling first.
    • Missing your stop or your level because you were reading news costs real money. The chart is where your money is.

    News days: hands off, then follow

    On days with a big release (CPI, jobs, Fed), you know the time in advance from your Sunday list. Here's the whole routine for an 8:30 release:

    8:25Close or tighten your trade
    8:30–8:32Hands off. Price whips both ways
    8:32–9:00Watch the 2-yr yield and the dollar. Which way do they hold?
    AfterTrade gold in the direction they point
    If the 2-yr yield and dollar go up and stay up

    Gold likely goes down

    Look for shorts, or skip longs.
    If the 2-yr yield and dollar go down and stay down

    Gold likely goes up

    Look for longs, or skip shorts.

    Check your prop firm's news rule first. Some don't allow trades a few minutes before and after these releases.

    WHY
    • Why hands off for 2 minutes: in the first seconds many buyers and sellers pull their orders and computer programs trade the headline number before anyone reads the details. Price jumps both ways and stops fill at bad prices (slippage).
    • Why watch the 2-yr yield and the dollar: news reaches gold through rates and the dollar. The 2-yr is the market's live vote on what the Fed will do. If it holds its move for 15–30 minutes, big money believes the news.
    • Why follow their direction: you're trading with the chain reaction from slide 1, not guessing.

    Does this change my bias?

    Your bias holds all week unless one of these five things happens. Answer each question. If all five are "No", everything else was noise.

    Set TradingView price alerts on TVC:DXY, TVC:US02Y and your gold weekly levels. Then the alerts tell you when to ask these questions, and you don't have to keep checking.

    WHY
    • Each trigger changes one of two things: what it costs to hold gold (questions 1–3) or how scared the world is (question 4). Question 5 is different: it shows real money has already changed sides.
    • The limits (15 bp, 1.5%) are bigger than a normal week's wobble, so they filter out noise.
    • If you re-scored on every headline, you'd flip direction all the time. You'd buy after rises and sell after drops, which is exactly backwards.

    Once a month, and every 50 trades

    Two bigger check-ups keep you on track. You don't need to do them more often.

    Once a month · 30 min
    • Which driver is gold really following this month?
    • Are central banks still buying? (World Gold Council)
    • What does next month's seasonality say? (step 7)
    Every 50 trades · 1 hour
    trade 12550 → review
    • Which setups made money? Keep them.
    • Which lost? Stop trading them.
    • Did you follow your plan? (step 10)
    WHY
    • Monthly: the driver in charge changes slowly, over months. Central banks becoming the main buyer in 2022 is an example. Checking monthly catches that without chasing noise.
    • 50 trades: a few trades prove nothing. Flip a coin 10 times and you can easily get 7 heads by luck. With about 50 trades, real patterns start to show over luck, so you can trust what the numbers say.

    Check yourself

    Your bias is long. Monday morning the dollar is up 0.9% overnight and the 2-yr yield is up 12 bp. What do you do?

    Both numbers moved a lot against a long bias. That's a red light. You don't have to flip short, but you shouldn't buy until you know whether the story changed.

    It's Tuesday at 10:45 and you're in a trade. A headline says a Fed official sounds worried about inflation. No alert has fired on the dollar or the 2-yr yield. What do you do?

    Your alerts are your filter. If the dollar and the 2-yr yield didn't break their levels, the headline didn't change the picture. Stick to the chart and your stop.

    RULE

    Plan on Sunday. Check three numbers each morning. Watch only the chart while trading. Re-score only when one of the five triggers fires.

    WHY

    Most of what you could read in a day doesn't change what it costs to hold gold. A fixed rhythm keeps your attention on the few things that do, and stops you from reacting to noise.

    Step 12 of 12 · Commitment of Traders

    Where should a retail trader stand?

    Every week the regulator shows who holds gold futures: hedge funds, banks, miners, and small traders like you. This lesson shows you how to read that report and use it to decide which side of the market to be on. Go through the 8 slides in order.

    Slide 1 of 8

    What the COT report is

    The CFTC (the US futures regulator) publishes the Commitment of Traders report every week. It's the only official look at how the big players are positioned in gold.

    TuesdayPositions are counted at the end of the day

    Gold is in the Disaggregated report, under "Gold – Commodity Exchange Inc.", CFTC code 088691. It's free at cftc.gov. On TradingView, add the indicator "Commitment of Traders: Disaggregated Metrics" to COMEX:GC1!. Holiday weeks can delay the release.

    WHY
    • Price shows you what happened. The COT shows you who did it. A rally driven by hedge funds buying is very different from one driven by traders closing shorts.
    • Because the data is 3 days old when you see it, it's a weekly bias tool. It tells you which side to favour, not when to enter.

    Every contract has two sides

    A futures contract is a deal between a buyer (long) and a seller (short). So all the longs in the market always add up to exactly all the shorts. If one group is heavily long, other groups must be short the same amount.

    Example numbers in the shape of a real report, simplified (spread positions left out).

    WHY
    • This is why the groups usually sit on opposite sides. When hedge funds pile into longs, those contracts are being sold to them by miners and banks.
    • So the question is never "who's right?" It's "who has room to keep buying or selling, and who might be forced to change?" That's what tells you where to stand.

    Meet the players

    The report splits traders into five groups. Each trades gold for a different reason, so each one's position means something different.

    Managed money

    Hedge funds and trend-following funds (CTAs). They trade to profit from price direction.

    Usually: net long, and it swings a lot.

    Tells you: which way the trend-drivers are leaning. This is the group to watch.

    Swap dealers

    Big banks. They sell gold exposure to clients and hedge it with futures.

    Usually: net short, a large amount.

    Tells you: the mirror of client demand. Their short is business, not a bet against gold.

    Producers & merchants

    Miners, refiners and jewellers. Miners sell future production to lock in a price.

    Usually: net short, more so when prices are high.

    Tells you: when they're least short, gold has often been near a low.

    Other reportables

    Large traders who don't fit the other groups, such as trading firms and family offices.

    Usually: mixed.

    Tells you: less useful on its own.

    YOU ARE HERESmall traders (nonreportable)

    Everyone below the CFTC's reporting size (200 contracts in gold). Individual traders and small funds.

    Usually: a small net long.

    Tells you: crowd mood at best. Small traders have tended to be most bullish near tops.

    If your prop account is simulated (many evaluation and some funded accounts are), your trades never reach the exchange and don't appear in the report. Either way, you trade at small-trader size. In the older "Legacy" report, commercials ≈ producers + swap dealers, and non-commercials ≈ managed money + other reportables.

    WHY
    • Only managed money trades gold mainly to profit from direction, and it moves in and out fast. Miners and banks mostly hedge. So you follow the funds, and you read the hedgers' shorts as business, not as a forecast.

    Is the room crowded? The COT index

    A net long of 155,000 means nothing on its own. Is that a lot? The COT index compares it with the last 3 years on a 0–100 scale.

    COT index = (this week − 3-yr lowest) ÷ (3-yr highest − 3-yr lowest) × 100
    0 · nobody long50100 · room is full
    COT index

    WHY
    • Think of a room. When it's full (index near 100), everyone who wanted to buy already has. There's nobody left to push price higher, and if bad news hits, they all rush for the door at once. That's when the sharpest drops happen.
    • When the room is empty (near 0), there's lots of buying power waiting on the sidelines. Even small good news can bring them back in.

    Check yourself

    The report shows swap dealers (banks) net short 160,000 gold contracts. What does that most likely mean?

    Banks sell gold exposure to clients and hedge it by shorting futures. Their short grows when client demand grows. It isn't a directional bet, so don't copy it.

    The managed money COT index is 92. This week funds cut 20,000 net longs and gold fell. Where should you stand?

    A full room that starts to empty means lots of longs may still need to sell. Don't be the last buyer. Protect profits, and only consider shorts if price breaks support.

    The COT index is 45, funds added longs this week, and gold rose. Where should you stand?

    The funds are buying, there's plenty of room left, and price agrees. That's the best place for a retail trader: riding with the money that's still pushing.

    RULE

    In the middle of a move, stand with the funds. At the extremes, never be the last one in. Always wait for price to agree.

    WHY

    You can't move gold, so you win by riding with the money that can. The COT shows where that money is and whether it has room left. It's 3 days old when you see it, so it sets your bias and your chart handles the timing.
