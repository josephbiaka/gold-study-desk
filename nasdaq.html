    Price = earnings × the multiple people will pay

    Every fundamental question about NQ comes back to two things: what the big companies will earn, and what multiple the market will pay for those earnings. Rates and the Fed set the multiple. Earnings and guidance set the earnings. This lab teaches you to read both every week.

    Check yourself

    You hold 3 MNQ and NQ drops 150 points against you. What's your loss?

    MNQ pays $2 a point: 150 × $2 × 3 = $900. The same move on 3 NQ would be $9,000.

    RULE

    Know how many NQ points your daily limit can absorb before you trade. If it's less than one normal hour's range, you're too big.

    WHY

    NQ can move 100+ points in minutes after CPI or a mega-cap report. If your limit only survives a 50-point move, noise ends your day before your fundamental read has a chance to play out.

    Step 2 of 9 · The index

    NQ is a bet on a handful of giant companies

    The Nasdaq-100 holds the 100 largest non-financial companies listed on Nasdaq, weighted by size. A few mega-caps dominate it, so their earnings matter more than the other 90 combined.

    Learn

    Why concentration changes your analysis

    • The top 10 holdings are roughly half the index. The exact weights shift every quarter; check the current list on Nasdaq's or Invesco's QQQ holdings page.
    • It's mostly technology and tech-like businesses: chips, software and cloud, internet platforms, plus consumer names such as Amazon and Tesla. Banks are excluded by rule.
    • These are "long-duration" companies. Much of their value comes from profits expected years from now, so they react strongly to interest rates (step 3).
    • AI spending is the current engine. Chipmakers sell to the cloud giants. When the cloud giants raise capex plans, chip stocks rise. When investors question the payoff, both can fall together.

    Check yourself

    A stock that is 8% of the index falls 10% after earnings. With NQ at 28,000, roughly how far does that one stock pull NQ down?

    28,000 × 8% × 10% ≈ 224 points, which is $4,480 on one NQ contract. One report can make the whole week's move.

    RULE

    Before each week, know which top-10 companies report and when. Their reports are index events, not stock events.

    WHY

    With heavy concentration, a single report after the close can gap NQ 1–2% overnight. Holding through one without knowing it was coming is gambling.

    Step 3 of 9 · The multiple

    Rates decide what each dollar of earnings is worth

    Index = forward earnings per share × forward P/E. Earnings change slowly. The P/E can change every day, and the 10-year real yield is its biggest driver.

    Learn

    Three numbers to track

    Forward P/E
    Price divided by the next 12 months of expected earnings. High means expectations are already rich, so disappointments hurt more. Sources disagree on the exact figure (FactSet, Bloomberg and free sites use different estimates), so use one source consistently and watch its direction.
    Earnings yield
    The P/E flipped: 1 ÷ P/E. A P/E of 22 means a 4.5% earnings yield. This is what stocks "pay" you in profit per dollar invested.
    Real yield
    The 10-year Treasury yield after inflation (FRED: DFII10). It's what a risk-free bond pays you in real terms. When it rises, future profits are discounted harder and growth stocks lose value.
    Equity risk premium
    Earnings yield − real yield. The extra return you get for owning stocks over safe bonds. A shrinking gap means stocks are getting expensive relative to bonds.

    Check yourself

    Earnings estimates for the Nasdaq-100 rose 2% this week, but the 10-year real yield jumped 30bp. What usually wins for NQ in the short run?

    With an assumed −3 P/E points per +1% real yield, a 30bp jump takes about 0.9 off a 22 P/E, roughly −4%. That's double the 2% earnings gain. Over months earnings matter more; week to week, rates usually move the multiple faster.

    RULE

    Check the 10-year real yield before you form a weekly bias. A sharp rise is a headwind for NQ even when the earnings news is good.

    WHY

    Nasdaq companies are valued on profits far in the future. Higher real yields shrink the present value of those profits, so the multiple falls even when the businesses are doing fine.

    Step 4 of 9 · The earnings

    Guidance moves the index more than the beat

    Most big companies beat estimates most quarters, so a beat is expected. The market reacts to what they say about the next quarter, and for the AI trade, to how much they plan to spend.

    Learn

    Earnings season rhythm

    • Four seasons a year, starting about two weeks after each quarter ends: mid-January, mid-April, mid-July, mid-October. The big platform and cloud companies usually report in the last week of the first month.
    • Nvidia reports about a month later (late February, May, August and November), so it often acts as a second, separate earnings event for the whole AI complex.
    • Reports come after 16:00 ET. NQ reacts in the evening session. The conference call an hour later, with guidance and capex comments, often reverses the first move.
    • What to read in each report: revenue growth vs expectations, next-quarter guidance, margins, cloud growth (Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, AWS), AI capex plans, and for chipmakers, data-centre revenue and supply comments.

    Check yourself

    A chipmaker beats revenue and EPS, but guides next quarter in line with estimates. The stock had rallied 25% into the report. The most common reaction?

    After a big run, the market needs a raise in guidance to justify it. "Beat and in-line" after a 25% rally is a classic sell-the-news setup, and it can drag the rest of the chip group with it.

    RULE

    Don't hold a full-size NQ position through a top-10 company's report. Go flat, or cut to a size where a 2% overnight gap is acceptable.

    WHY

    The reaction depends on guidance and positioning you can't know in advance. A beat can still fall 10%. Your edge is reading the reaction after it happens, not guessing it.

    Step 5 of 9 · The Fed

    Read every release through the Fed's eyes

    Economic data moves NQ mainly by changing what the Fed is expected to do. The same strong number can be good or bad for tech, depending on whether inflation is the Fed's problem.

    Learn

    Check yourself

    The Fed is hiking to fight inflation. Retail sales come in much stronger than expected. What's the most likely first reaction in NQ?

    In an overheating regime, strong growth data raises the odds of more hikes. Yields rise and the multiple compresses. "Good news is bad news" until inflation is under control.

    RULE

    Before each release, write down which regime you're in and what a hot and a cold number would mean. Then trade the reaction, not the headline.

    WHY

    The first spike after 08:30 is often reversed once traders look at the details (core vs headline, revisions). Having the map ready keeps you from chasing the first candle.

    Step 6 of 9 · Confirmation

    Ask bonds, the dollar and chips whether they agree

    NQ rarely moves alone. When the markets that feed it agree, a move tends to last. When they disagree, be suspicious.

    Check yourself

    NQ makes a new weekly high, but SOX is down on the day and the 10-year yield is up 8bp. What's the best read?

    Divergence is a reason to wait, not a reason to reverse. Without chips and rates confirming, breakouts fail more often. Wait for them to line up, or trade smaller.

    RULE

    Only take a full-size trade in your bias direction when at least two of yields, chips and the dollar agree with it.

    WHY

    A fundamental bias says which way the wind blows. Intermarket confirmation tells you whether it's blowing today.

    Step 7 of 9 · The crowd

    Fundamentals set direction; crowding sets the speed

    When everyone already owns NQ, good news has less fuel. When everyone is scared, bad news has less effect. Use sentiment as a contrarian filter, not a signal by itself.

    Learn

    Four gauges

    VIX level
    Under about 15: calm, complacent. 20–30: stressed. Over 30: panic, and often near a short-term low.
    VIX term structure
    Compare VIX with VIX3M (3-month). Normally VIX is lower. When VIX rises above VIX3M, fear is acute right now, which often happens near lows.
    COT (Nasdaq-100)
    The CFTC's Friday report. Watch asset managers (slow, long-term money) and leveraged funds (fast money). Extreme leveraged-fund positioning, either way, often reverses.
    Surveys & put/call
    AAII bull/bear survey and the equity put/call ratio. Extremes are contrarian: very bullish readings warn of a pullback, very bearish ones of a bounce.

    Check yourself

    Your fundamentals are bearish (real yields surging), but VIX is 34 and above VIX3M, and the AAII survey is deeply bearish. What do you do?

    Panic readings mean much of the bad news is already in price, and bounces get violent. The fundamental bias stays, but the timing says sell rallies rather than chase lows.

    RULE

    Sentiment can reduce your size or delay your entry. It should never flip your fundamental bias on its own.

    WHY

    Extremes can last for weeks. Using sentiment as a timing filter keeps you from shorting the hole or buying the top without fighting the bigger drivers.

    Check yourself

    Your score is −35: rates and the Fed are strongly negative, earnings are mildly positive. What does that mean for the week?

    A moderate score is a lean, not a conviction. It tells you which side to favour and how hard to press. Strong scores (beyond ±60) justify fuller size; scores near zero mean trade both ways small, or wait.

    RULE

    Write down your score and its main reason every Sunday. Only change it mid-week if a Tier 1 event changes a driver.

    WHY

    A written bias stops you from flipping direction on every candle. Reviewing old scorecards against what NQ did is how you learn which drivers you read well.

    Your routine checklist

    Check yourself

    Buffer $2,000, risk 10% ($200), MNQ, ATR 40 points, stop 1.5× ATR. How many MNQ can you trade?

    60 points × $2 = $120 risk per MNQ. $200 ÷ $120 = 1.67, rounded down to 1. On an event day, halve it, which means 0: sit out or wait for volatility to settle.

    RULE

    Size from your stop in points × dollars per point. Round down. Halve it on CPI, FOMC and top-10 earnings days.

    WHY

    NQ's daily range is often 300+ points. Without volatility-based sizing, one normal swing against you can take your whole daily limit.
